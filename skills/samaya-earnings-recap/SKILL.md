# Earnings Call Debrief

Shared rules — the connectivity gate, consensus, citation, traceability, the attention fill,
desk, authorship and the common validation gates — live in `references/samaya-conventions.md`,
and the page-one layout in `references/document-architecture.md`, and the writing standard — how every line is worded — in
`references/samaya-house-language.md`. Read all three before the first retrieval; each section
below keeps its non-negotiables and points there for the full rule.

This debriefs the results event as a whole — the release and the call together.
The release carries the numbers; the call carries the reasoning, the hedging, the
pushback and the things management chose not to say. The release is reported in its
own right, and the call's contribution is stated as the delta against what the print
already told you — with anything drawn from the release labelled as such, so the
reader can see which findings needed the call.

The output is a debrief for someone who could not attend, or who attended and
wants the second read. It is not a transcript summary, and it never passes a figure
already in the release off as something the call revealed.

**The deliverable is the differentiated finding, not coverage of the event.** The reader can get
a summary anywhere. What they cannot get anywhere is the one or two things said on this call
that change how the business should be understood — a diagnosis restated from market-driven to
self-inflicted, a price position conceded, a reinvestment implied but not quantified. Those are
the report. Everything else is context for them, and context belongs behind them.

**Language is evidence, not colour.** A beat can trade down because the tone turned, and a miss
can trade up because the explanation narrowed. Treat what management said, and how the saying
changed since last quarter, with the same seriousness as what they printed — and put both in
front of the reader in the same form, as tables they can scan rather than paragraphs they must
mine.

**Length is a cost, not a virtue.** This report earns its place by being read in full on the way
into a morning meeting. See the length budget below: it is a hard constraint, not an aspiration,
and a section that cannot justify its space is cut rather than shortened.

## STEP 0 — SAMAYA CONNECTIVITY GATE (hard requirement, before anything else)

Read `references/samaya-conventions.md` first — it carries the full rule for this and for every
shared section below. Then: confirm `financial_qa` and `financial_qa_result` are in the tool list
(match the tool name, not the client's prefix; if absent, stop and ask the analyst to connect
Samaya); fire one trivial test query and get a real answer before building; on failure retry the
same session, then a fresh one, then report and pause — never a silent fallback to web search or
any other source; any non-Samaya source needs the analyst's explicit approval, per source, and is
labelled with its true retrieval path. An uploaded transcript alone is a build source only where the user elected the upload path in Step 0b.

## STEP 0b — SUBJECT AND SOURCE

Resolve in one short turn. Do not proceed on a guess for the first two.

1. **Company and period.** Resolve the ticker to its legal name via Samaya.
   Confirm which call: the quarter or half, and the date it was held.
2. **Transcript source.** The call transcript retrieved through Samaya, or a
   transcript the user uploads. Where the retrieved transcript is partial,
   say which portions are missing before building anything on them.
3. **Consensus provider — blocking, and never defaulted.** Ask for the provider and
   the vintage date before anything is retrieved: Visible Alpha, a company-compiled
   consensus from the investor relations page, another mapped feed, or a file the
   analyst supplies. **Do not default to any provider.** Two runs on the same company
   days apart have landed on different providers because nobody chose, and the two
   reports then disagreed about whether the print missed, on the same reported number.
   Where the company itself points to a compiled consensus, say so and offer it, since
   that is usually the one the market is quoting.
   **The provider and its vintage date are printed in the consensus column header and
   in the event strip**, so a reader sees what the comparison is against without
   opening the appendix. Never blend providers, within a line or across the document.
4. **Comparison baseline.** Tone, certainty language and the key metrics are read
   against a trailing baseline — the reported period and the prior three comparable
   periods. Confirm the trailing set; default to the three immediately preceding
   comparable calls and periods.

## BUILD MAP

Work through the elected sections in order. Write each section to the report as
it completes; a run stopped part way should leave a usable debrief, not nothing.

1. Ground the event: the call date, participants by role, format, whether the
   transcript is complete, and the release and guide in force. This is retrieval
   for your own use and it renders as the four-line provenance strip, not as a
   section. Detail beyond those four lines goes to the appendix.
2. Split the transcript into prepared remarks and question and answer before
   reading either. Record where the split falls.
3. Pull the release and the guide in force so the delta can be established; label
   release-sourced findings as such.
4. Build the elected numbers work: consensus, actuals, guide, and the trailing
   four-period table — reported plus the prior three comparable periods.
5. Build the elected segment work: per-segment driver split where disclosed,
   structural shift, and geography — never inferring volume from revenue and price.
6. Read the prepared remarks against the trailing three-period baseline; mark
   inflection points only where a dated change in direction is evidenced.
7. Read the question and answer: cluster by theme, count questioners per theme,
   then score answers.
8. Build "what to watch next": dated catalysts mapped to the thesis, named
   sensitivities, channel and order-book commentary, and exactly five questions.
9. Assemble "what the call changed" last among the sections, since it draws on
   all of them.
10. Write the conclusion.
11. Run the self-check.

If a retrieval block returns nothing, write the section with the gap named and
move on. Never stall on one line.

## CONSENSUS — provider-agnostic, declared once, never assumed

Ask once at kickoff, in the same message as everything else: `Consensus source? Visible Alpha /
Bloomberg / FactSet / company-compiled / a file you supply / none`. Never default to a provider;
where the analyst states no preference, use what the session can reach and say which. Record the
provider and its as-of date on every consensus figure. One provider per comparison, never blended; match the definition before
calling a gap a gap; never infer a segment estimate from a headline one or present one broker's
estimate as consensus. No consensus is a legitimate answer: the run completes with those cells
blank and the attempt recorded. Full rule: `references/samaya-conventions.md` § CONSENSUS.

## RETRIEVAL AND CITATION

Route every pull by what the item is:

- **The call itself** — the transcript via Samaya, or the uploaded file.
- **Reported actuals, guidance language, segment figures** — the release,
  presentation and filings via Samaya.
- **Consensus** — the elected source, as of a stated date.
- **Market data** — as of a stated date, and labelled as market data.
- **Prior calls, for comparison work** — transcripts via Samaya, each dated.

Every figure and every quotation carries its source and its as-of date. Where a
consensus vintage is stale relative to the call, say how stale.

## DESK — established, never assumed

Sell-side, buy-side and sales and trading get the same content; only emphasis and order change.
Establish the desk from context; ask once, in the kickoff message, only where genuinely unclear;
otherwise build in the neutral order and say so in the header. Never fork the artefact per desk.
Never render an empty value — omit the row and name it once. Run the defaults, then say what else
exists: only the blocking kickoff item blocks. Full rule: `references/samaya-conventions.md`
§ DESK.

## TRACEABILITY — everything clicks back, and nothing is cited twice

Excel **Notes**, never threaded comments; every Note carries its Samaya link as plain text, in
the form `https://app.samaya.ai/app/chat/{chatId}?artifactId={artifactId}&referencesVisible=true`,
written exactly as returned. Cite the retrieved figure, not every cell downstream; one link per
retrieval, reused; do not re-cite within a block; never fabricate a link. Prose about a workbook
links to the cells it names with `=HYPERLINK("#'<Sheet>'!<Cell>", "<label>")`, resolved by row
label at runtime. Full rule: `references/samaya-conventions.md` § TRACEABILITY.

## ATTENTION CLASSIFICATION — settled, or needs the analyst's eye

Every line touched is one of three, and the Read says which: **settled — reported** (no fill,
linked to source); **needs review — estimate** (attention fill plus a one-line Note saying why);
**needs review — mechanics** (attention fill plus a Note naming the concern). The attention fill
is the only fill, so it needs no legend; if more than a tenth of the touched lines want it, say so
in the Read instead of colouring the block. Full rule: `references/samaya-conventions.md`
§ ATTENTION CLASSIFICATION.

## AUTHORSHIP AND HOUSE STYLE

Authorship is Samaya AI, never the operator: `Samaya AI` on every Note, on the document

## DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE — fixed, and identical in every run

Masthead → headline (one declarative sentence naming the finding, written last) → the takeaway →
key-numbers tiles → contents (where more than three sections ran) → body in menu order → appendix
→ conclusion. Nothing that reports an absence appears above the body; the provenance strip is six
labelled items at most and the only block above the takeaway; a source-class legend on page one
and a running footer on every page. Full layout and the page-one gates:
`references/document-architecture.md`. The strip here is: event,
participants and format; comparison basis; source completeness — four labelled lines, with
transcript renderings that disagree, figures omitted for conflict and market-data as-of lines in
the appendix.

## THE TAKEAWAY — page one, and it states what is new

**The first sentence names what this call changed, not what this report covers.** A takeaway that
opens by describing the event, the quarter or the structure of the document has wasted the only
lines guaranteed to be read. The test is simple: **could this sentence have been written from the
release alone?** If yes, it is not the takeaway — find the thing that needed the call.

Where the differentiated finding is qualitative, lead with it in the speaker's own words and then
say what it implies. A conceded diagnosis, a reframed cause, a position defended more weakly than
last quarter: these lead over a beat.

## CONCLUSION — mandatory, five sentences, always present

Every run closes with a five-sentence conclusion, regardless of scope, sections
or depth. Prose, no bullets. Each sentence has a job:

1. **What the call changed** — the single most consequential difference between
   what was known before the call and what is known after it.
2. **Answered most fully** — the theme answered with the most specificity, and what
   made it specific.
3. **Not answered** — the theme deflected, declined or answered thinly, named with
   the count of times it was raised.
4. **What the call did to the guide** — whether the guide hardened, softened or
   held, and where it now sits against consensus.
5. **What settles the open question** — the disclosure or event that resolves the
   thing the call left open, with its date where scheduled.

Write it last, from the sections above. Where the transcript was partial,
sentence one says so before anything else. It reports a call; it issues no
rating, target or recommendation.

## OUTPUT

Markdown report, built to the document architecture above without deviation:
masthead, headline, takeaway, contents, body, appendix, conclusion. Tables for
the numbers against the print, the trailing four-period frame and the segment
decomposition; prose for the question-and-answer read, where the surrounding
context is what makes an answer's quality legible. The appendix carries the
transcript provenance and any missing portions, consensus vintage and staleness,
market-data as-of lines, and the not-run list.

## SCOPE

Runs on a single listed company's results event — the release and the call
together — any sector, any listing, any period. Works from a transcript retrieved
through Samaya or one the user uploads, with the release and prior periods pulled
through Samaya. Reports the reported numbers on a trailing frame and what the call
changed against them. It does not value the company,
does not build or edit a model, and does not issue a rating, a target or a
recommendation.
