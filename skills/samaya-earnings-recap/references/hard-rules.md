# Hard rules

Moved out of `SKILL.md` to keep it lean; read it before the first retrieval.

## HARD RULES

1. **The results event is the unit; the call's contribution is the delta.** The
   release is reported in the numbers section, not excluded. State the call's
   contribution as the delta against what was known from the release before the
   call began, and label anything drawn from the release as such — so the reader
   can see which findings needed the call and which were already in the print.

2. **Prepared remarks and question and answer are separate corpora.** Never
   merge them, never average across them, never attribute a scripted line to the
   live portion or the reverse. A commitment made in script and a commitment made
   under questioning carry different weight, and the report says which.

3. **Answer quality is scored against the question asked.** A fluent answer to a
   different question is a deflection, not an answer. Score on whether the thing
   asked was addressed: answered, partially answered, deflected, or declined.
   Quote the question and the operative part of the answer where the score is
   anything other than answered.

4. **Attribute to role, never to an outside individual.** Management statements
   carry the speaker's role and the date. Questions from the line are attributed
   to the broker where identifiable, never to a named individual analyst.

5. **Certainty language is matched, not upgraded.** "Management expects" is not
   "management confirmed". "We would hope to" is not guidance. Carry the modal
   verb the speaker used.

6. **Guidance is quoted, not paraphrased into a number.** Where management guides
   to a range or a qualitative direction, the report carries the words. Any
   derived figure is labelled as derived and its arithmetic shown.

7. **A theme needs more than one questioner to be a theme.** A single question is
   a question. Say which it is, and give the count.

8. **Silence is a finding, stated as silence.** Where the print invited a
   question that nobody asked, or management declined to answer, record it
   plainly. Do not speculate about motive.

9. **No rating, target or recommendation.** This skill reports a call. Where the
   user supplies their own rating and target, the report may state whether the
   call supports or strains the thesis as the user has described it, and no more.

10. **Blank rather than estimate.** Where a figure, a consensus vintage or a
    portion of the transcript is not retrievable, leave it blank, name what
    failed, and continue. Never substitute a nearby period, never carry a figure
    forward, never reconstruct a quotation from memory.

11. **Quotations short and sparse.** Enough to carry the point, never enough to
    substitute for the transcript.

12. **Citation is mandatory on every retrieved item** and does not depend on any
    citation instruction being present elsewhere in the session. Each item's
    source line reads `Samaya — [document] ([date])` plus the resolvable link. A
    source line without a link is incomplete. Items from an uploaded transcript
    are attributed plainly by file name, no link.

13. **Trailing-period comparison, always.** This is a running requirement, not an
    optional section. The numbers work carries the reported period plus the prior
    three comparable periods — revenue, margin, EPS and growth — as a table.
    Management tone and certainty language are read against the same trailing
    three-period baseline, so a sentiment change is stated against the trailing
    frame rather than only against the immediately prior call.

14. **Segment driver splits are disclosed, never constructed.** Give the pricing,
    mix and volume split only where the company discloses it; where it does not,
    say so rather than building one. Never infer volume from revenue and price
    unless both are given for the identical period.
