## GUIDED METRICS INVENTORY — three tables, not prose

The section renders as three compact tables, each followed by a short Read. **A paragraph carrying
more than three figures is a build failure in this section.**

1. **Metrics currently guided** — one row per metric: the metric, the horizon (quarterly /
   half-year / full-year / medium-term), the convention it is guided on (organic, constant-FX,
   reported), and the format it is guided in.
2. **Metrics added or dropped over the window** — one row per change: the metric, whether it was
   added or dropped, and the period it changed. **This table carries the finding**, because a
   metric quietly dropped is usually one management stopped being confident about. It is never
   omitted; where nothing was added or dropped over the window it says so in a single row.
3. **Guidance format by metric** — one row per metric: range, point, "approximately", directional,
   or floor.

Each table is followed by a two-to-four-sentence Read. The added-or-dropped table's Read names what
left the guide and when, and feeds the "what left the guide" section below.

## THE REVISION-PATH CHART — exactly one chart, drawn from the table

The revision path section carries **exactly one chart**: per year, the initial guide, each reset in
sequence, the final guide in force, and what was delivered, with the metric and unit on the axis
and the guide vintages visually distinguishable from the outcome. Where the window covers several
metrics, chart the one the company leads on and say why; where the analyst named a metric at
kickoff, chart theirs.

- It sits with the revision path section, never on page one — the key-numbers tiles already carry
  page one.
- It is drawn from the same retrieved figures as the table beneath it, never from a separately
  recalled series. Where the two disagree, the table is right and the chart is rebuilt.
- It is followed by a Read interpreting the shape: a low initial guide walked up, a high one walked
  down, or a stable guide held.
- Where the output surface cannot render a chart, say so in one line in the appendix and let the
  table stand alone. Never substitute ASCII art.

## VOCABULARY — both terms glossed at first use in the output

Both terms carry the analysis, and both are glossed at first use in the output, in a half-sentence
each, in the clause where they first appear — never in a separate glossary the reader has to look
for.

- **Guide in force** — the last standing guidance before the period closed, after every revision;
  scoring against it asks whether management hit the number they were pointing at by the end.
- **Initial guide** — the first guidance given for that period, before any revision; scoring
  against it asks whether management could see the year from the start.

The gap between the two is the finding, so a reader who does not know which is which cannot read the
report.

## WHAT LEFT THE GUIDE — the largest credibility item may not be in the scorecards

A target withdrawn before it could be missed never appears as a miss. A medium-term frame
reconfirmed and then replaced, an annual metric guided for two years and then dropped, a number
that becomes a direction — none of these score in either table, and any one can be a larger gap
between what a company committed to and what it delivered than every scored row combined.

Before the verdict is written, establish what left the guide. Where the answer is material it leads
— in the takeaway and on page one, not discovered at the back under the verdict. Where the largest
item in the record is a disappearance rather than a miss, the report says so in the headline. The
scorecards then read as the record of what remained scoreable.
