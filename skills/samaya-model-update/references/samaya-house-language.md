# Samaya house language — shared by every Samaya skill

How every Samaya deliverable is written — findings, headings, lead-ins, callout labels, table
cells, takeaways and the conclusion, in reports, decks, workbook Notes and plain chat alike. A
skill's `SKILL.md` points here; this file is the full rule. Read it before writing any output.

**Where a rule below and a worked example disagree, the example wins.** The abstract rule did not
hold on its own in practice; the examples are what make it checkable.

## 1. No sayings

**Write the finding. Do not arrange it.** The test: **would this survive being read aloud to the
company without sounding written?** If it sounds composed, rewrite it as a statement.

Banned in every position:

- **Balanced clause pairs** arranged for rhythm rather than meaning.
- **"X, not Y" in any form** — in lead-ins, callout labels, table cells and Reads, not only
  titles. It asserts a contrast instead of showing one, and it is the most frequent offender.
- **Puns, metaphors and borrowed imagery.** Businesses do not have engines and engines do not get
  starved. Lines do not carry reports. Figures do not land on top of things.
- **Callout labels that describe the document.** A callout is labelled by what it says.
- **Phrases that sound like meaning and carry none.** Where a quotation already says the thing,
  the framing sentence is cut.

| Banned | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| The release cut the guide; the call put the price on it. | The release cut the guide. The call gave the cost: €100m of extra H2 media. |
| The April position was reversed, not refined. | In April management said the budget was stable. In August they added €100m. |
| What needed the call was the cost. | The cost was only given on the call. |
| The growth engines are being starved. | Investment in the two growing brands fell 4% while the declining one absorbed the increase. |
| The line that carries the report. *(callout label)* | 2027 margin holds at the 2026 level. *(callout label)* |
| The half landed on top of consensus. | Sales and group EBIT margin were both slightly ahead, so the news was the guidance cut. |

**Plain contrasts are fine and are not the banned construction.** "Partly the brand itself rather
than just sell-in" says both things. "X, not Y" asserts a contrast for effect. The difference is
whether both halves carry information.

## 2. Use the ordinary word

Academic and legal vocabulary does not appear: **falsify, adjudicate, epistemic, dispositive,
salient** and their relatives.

| Banned | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| falsify, unfalsifiable | disprove, cannot be checked |
| houses *(for the firms asking questions)* | brokers |
| adjudicate | settle, decide |
| salient | important, relevant |
| dispositive | decisive, settles it |

"House" stays correct in **house label**, **house style**, **house format**, **house standard**
and **house convention** — those are Samaya's own conventions. Never replace those.

## 3. Do not grade anyone

**Score the answer against the question asked. Do not grade the answerer.** One is an assessment
of evidence, the other is an opinion about people, and a report the company may read contains only
the first.

Permitted verdicts wherever a response or disclosure is assessed:

**Answered most fully · Answered · Partially answered · Answered a different question ·
Deflected · Declined · Not answered**

| Banned | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| The theme handled worst | Not answered: the sun care contribution, asked twice |
| Where management was strongest | Answered most fully |
| Management failed to quantify | The figure was not given |
| Handled badly, fumbled, evasive | Deflected · Answered a different question |

This applies to every skill that scores or ranks anything. Anywhere a table is ordered by how well
something was handled, the column header carries the neutral wording. Never attribute motive — to
management, regulators, brokers or anyone else.

## 4. Never address the reader, and no glossary

No "what the reader needs to know", no "four things a listener knows now", no "you will see
below". Analysts do not write that way and it reads as a document describing itself.

**No glossary and no defining ordinary terms.** A line explaining that organic growth strips out
currency, or that EBIT excluding special factors is operating profit before one-offs, is written
for someone who is not the audience and costs the space the finding needed. Gloss only what is
genuinely company-specific, in the clause where it appears.

## 5. Numbers and signs

- **Numbers belong in cells.** A paragraph carrying more than three figures is a table that has
  not been built yet. This holds on page one as much as in the body.
- **Signs, not words.** Write `-3.5%`, never minus 3.5%. In reports, in tables and in prose.
  Parentheses for negatives remain the convention in workbooks, which are a different surface.
- **Margins and growth rates in basis points.**
- **No em dashes in a takeaway or any chat-voice block.**
- **Do not write beat or miss** where a difference column already shows it.
- **Every figure in a summary says what it implies.** A figure on page one carries its consequence
  in the same sentence — for estimates, for the balance sheet, or for what happens next — or it
  belongs in a table in the body instead. A summary that lists what happened without saying what
  follows from it has described the period and left the reader to do the work.

| Banned | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| The company spent $212m on buybacks in H1 against $1,653m a year earlier. | Buybacks fell to $212m in H1 from $1,653m a year earlier, so reaching the $2bn plan needs $1.79bn in H2 against about $3bn of free cash flow and $2.6bn of dividends. |

## 6. Layout

- **No section or table is split across a page break.** A heading on one page with its table on
  the next is a layout failure. Where a block will not fit in the space left, the whole block
  moves to the next page. A table that must break repeats its header row; one that would break
  with two rows left moves whole.
- **No prose introduces a table**, or where it does, it accounts for every line that matters
  including the ones that went the other way. A sentence naming what beat and omitting what missed
  is a partial summary and is worse than no sentence. **Never claim a comparison the table does
  not show.**
- **Every table is followed by a short paragraph saying what it means**, two to four sentences,
  interpreting rather than restating. A table with no reading is an unfinished section.
- **Nothing reporting an absence appears above the body.** Method, coverage, what could not be
  retrieved and the not-run list all go in the appendix.
- **Never render an empty value.** A missing figure says not disclosed or not retrievable, in the
  cell, and the row stays.
- **Cite with superscript numbers to a numbered source list** where the surface supports it.
  Whole sentences are never hyperlinked: a link under a sentence pulls the eye out of the line.
  One number per source per section. In workbooks, sourcing stays as Notes with their links.

## 7. The materiality pass

Run after the body is built and before handing anything back. No retrieval, no new content. It
only promotes, converts, demotes and cuts:

1. **Promote** the two or three most material findings to page one, wherever they ended up.
2. **Convert** any paragraph carrying a comparison or more than three figures into a table.
3. **Demote** method, coverage and anything reporting an absence to the appendix.
4. **Cut** sentences that restate a table, and sections that survived on completeness rather than
   content.
5. **Rewrite the title last**, from what now sits at the top. A title stating a circumstance of
   the event rather than a finding about the business has described the setup and not the news.
6. **Sweep all four positions** — headings, lead-ins, callout labels and table cells — against
   sections 1 to 3: read each aloud, cut anything arranged for effect, replace any academic word,
   and check nothing grades anyone.

The test: **would someone reading only page one have the finding?**
