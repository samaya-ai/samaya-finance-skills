## LAYOUT — what the page has to do

**No section or table is split across a page break.** A heading on one page with its table on the
next is a layout failure. Where a block will not fit in the space left, the whole block moves to
the next page. A table that must break repeats its header row, and one that would break with two
rows left is moved whole.

**Prose above a table accounts for every line that matters, or there is no prose.** A sentence
that names the two entries that support the point and omits the one that does not is a partial
summary, and it is worse than no sentence. **Never claim a comparison the table does not show.**

**Where the window covers three years or more, chart the deployment.** One chart: cash out by use
per year — acquisitions, buybacks, dividends, capital spending — against cash in from disposals,
with a takeaway line above it and the same figures in a table underneath. Drawn from the retrieved
figures, never from a recalled series. Where the disclosure will not support the split, say so in
one line and run the table alone.

## SIDE-BY-SIDE COMPARISON — the default presentation wherever two figures are being compared

**Two figures being compared belong in adjacent columns of one table, with the difference in a
third.** Not in two tables, not in consecutive paragraphs, and not as a sentence asserting the
relationship. The reader's judgement is the deliverable here, and a comparison split across
paragraphs asks them to hold both numbers in their head and do the subtraction themselves. This is
the presentation default throughout the report, not a stylistic preference for one section.

The pairs this review produces, each rendered this way:

- **Multiple paid against the acquirer's own multiple** on the same basis at the announcement
  date, with the difference in turns, and the premium to the undisturbed price beside it where the
  target was listed.
- **Buyback average price paid against the average market price** over the identical period, with
  the difference in percent, and **programme authorised against amount executed** beside it.
- **Round trips: acquisition value against disposal value** for the same asset, with the holding
  period and the difference, both dates on the row.
- **Rationale at announcement against how the same transaction is described later**, the two
  verbatim and dated, in adjacent cells rather than separated by half a page.
- **Stated allocation framework, earlier wording against current wording**, where the framework
  section runs.
- **Announced value against final value** where consideration was revised between announcement
  and completion.

Four rules for these tables:

1. **The difference is computed in its own column, not narrated.** Where the delta column is
   self-evident, the Read does not restate it in words — it says what the pattern means.
2. **Units and basis stated once in the column header**, never repeated in every cell.
3. **Where one side of a pair is not retrievable, the row still appears** with the known figure
   and the missing side marked as not disclosed. Never drop the row and never leave a blank cell.
4. **Confirmed and reported never share a row**, here as everywhere — a reported figure is not
   comparable to a disclosed one and pairing them implies it is.

## THE LENGTH BUDGET

**Eight pages target, ten ceiling, appendix excluded.** A log grows with the window, so where the
analyst asks for a long window the event tables carry it and the prose stays the same length.
Electing an optional section displaces something rather than extending the report.

## THE MATERIALITY PASS — after the body is built

Re-read the whole document and reorder by what matters. No retrieval, no new content:

1. **Promote** the two or three most important findings to page one.
2. **Convert** any paragraph carrying a comparison or more than three figures into a table.
3. **Demote** method, coverage and anything about what is missing to the appendix.
4. **Cut** sentences that repeat a table and sections that survived on completeness alone.
5. **Rewrite the verdict line last**, from what now sits at the top.
6. **Sweep headings, lead-ins, callout labels and table cells** against the house language
   section: read each aloud, cut anything arranged for effect, and check nothing grades anyone.

The test: **would someone reading only page one know what the company did with its cash over the
window and which event mattered most?**
